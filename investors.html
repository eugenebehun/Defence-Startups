What the cap tables say

One accelerator dominates the count. Y Combinator appears on 22 of 46 rows — nearly half the list. That is not a normal sector distribution; it is the result of YC deliberately reversing its stance and admitting a defence cohort at scale. Treat it as a signal about YC, not about which companies are strongest.

Only one fund led twice. Plural led both Frankenburg Technologies and Hypersonica — both European missiles. Every other investor here led at most one round in the list. Concentration like that usually means conviction in a thesis rather than opportunism.

The institutional money arrived. Bain Capital, Accel, GV, Lux, Advent and JPMorgan are not venture tourists — their presence at Seed and Series A is what a late-cycle sector looks like. Alphabet's GV leading a $50M Series A for autonomous warships would have been unthinkable three years ago.

Two European funds were purpose-built for this. Lakestar closed Resilience I at €262.2M in July 2026 and Presto Tech Horizons raised €150M with Czechoslovak Group. Both exist only for defence and dual-use — new vehicles, not existing funds stretching their mandate.
